Harmonic Pulse

Kastle Drum → Wingie resonator

How it flows

The Kastle Drum fires short synthetic drum hits into the Wingie, a bank of 9 tuned resonators on each side. Each hit rings the resonators at musical pitches, so the drum becomes a melody. After that come the filters, colour (a post wave shaper), tape echo and reverb. The Voice adds a sine-to-pulse oscillator that plays the same melody, struck by each hit.

For melodic pulses, set a Wingie side to String or Bar. Tap a key to set its note, or hold Shift (or switch on Append) to add notes. Each hit louder than the threshold steps to the next note. Click a step to cycle its accent: normal, accent, ghost, rest. Right-click or long-press a step to remove it.

The Perform pad drives two knobs at once. Pick them with X → and Y ↑. Its X, Y and Gate jacks can be patched like any other output.

Patching

Drag from any jack to another jack, or click one and then the other. Square jacks are outputs and round jacks are inputs. Each input has a small attenuverter knob. Hold Alt and click a cable to unplug it, or double-click a jack to unplug everything on it.

TRIG is the Kastle's three-level input. Each change between low, mid and high fires a hit, and a one-step change is an accent. A square wave gives full hits on both edges, so ÷2 → TRIG plays 16ths in BPM lock. The triangle walks through all three levels, giving four accented hits per cycle.

Keys

Space hits the drum, Esc cancels patching and Shift-click appends a sequence note. You can drag knobs, scroll them or use the arrow keys, and double-click a knob to reset it. MIDI notes on channel 1 go to Wingie L, channel 2 to Wingie R, and channel 3 alternates between them. Channel 10 hits the drum.
